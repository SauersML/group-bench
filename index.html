FIELD NOTES

A map, with evidence.

Each property is a yes-or-no question about a countable discrete group. Your filters form a conjunction: every requirement must hold at once.

Exists means an actual example in the catalog is known to satisfy every requirement. Click its facts to trace the evidence.

Impossible means the requirements contradict sourced mathematical rules. Follow the conflicting requirements and deductions.

Unknown means neither certificate is available in this catalog. A consistent Boolean assignment is never treated as a group. Missing facts are never treated as false.

A black border identifies a documented open question, including all its hypotheses. Hover or click for the question, source, and review date. A missing border does not mean a question is settled. Literature review ↗

This is a partial classification. Sources are attached to definitions, example facts, and rules. Some examples link to independently checked formal proofs, identified in their provenance. The displayed deductions are not Lean-checked certificates.

Growth and algorithmic properties include finite generation in their definitions. “Simple” excludes the trivial group; “free” includes it. A group can have some torsion without being a torsion group.

Each example has a first-proof date field and source, with unknown dates stated explicitly. Construction, announcement, publication, and formal verification dates are distinguished. A date is not automatically applied to properties proved later, and an earliest recorded source does not by itself certify historical priority.

The plot counts each distinct pair once and excludes undated results from the curve. Its total is independent of the displayed grid. Both axes use logarithmic spacing to expand recent dates and low percentages, with zero included and 100% at the top. Hover on the plot for dates, or focus its line and use the arrow keys. Select a date to see its pairs and sources.

The map is a two-dimensional slice of a larger property space. Add any properties, including their “Does not have” versions. Both axes use the same selection. Only the upper triangle is shown because exchanging row and column gives the same conjunction. The diagonal is included. Add filters for requirements that apply to every cell. Links preserve your selections.
